## 1. SPIRITUAL JAZZ: DEFINITION & CHARACTERISTICS

### Core Definition

**Spiritual jazz** is fundamentally "an _approach_, not a sound" that uses jazz as a vehicle for spiritual and social expression. The genre emerged during the early 1960s as an expression of Black artistry during times of social struggle.

#### Key Characteristics (2020s Iteration)

- **Musical Foundation**: Draws from free jazz, avant-garde, and modal jazz forms
- **Instrumentation**: Extended ensembles featuring strings, choirs, harp synthesizers, and unconventional keyboards
- **Temporal Structure**: Long, open forms; drones and pedal points; polyrhythmic percussion
- **Sonic Range**: Can be "either dreamy and meditative or a free torrent of cacophony" 
- **Thematic Focus**: Transcendence and spirituality, often with explicit engagement with liberation politics and cultural identity
- **Global Reach**: Artists from Belgium, UK, Australia, Canada, and the United States (2020s expansion)
- **Compositional Approach**: Uncompromising intellectual exploration and search for meaning; incorporation of diverse musical traditions (European, Asian, African, Latin American)

**One-liner quotation**: 
> "Spiritual jazz is fundamentally an _approach_, not a sound—it emphasizes uncompromising intellectual exploration and a search for meaning through community connection and shared musical space."  
*Source: Bandcamp Daily Spiritual Jazz Album Guide*

### Historical Lineage

Spiritual jazz originated as an extension of 1960s avant-garde jazz. Key foundational figures:

| Artist | Key Work | Year | Role |
|--------|----------|------|------|
| John Coltrane | *A Love Supreme* | 1965 | Landmark album establishing spiritual transcendence in jazz |
| Alice Coltrane | Various | 1960s-70s | Piano/harp, spiritual orchestration |
| Pharoah Sanders | Collaborator with Coltrane | 1960s+ | Extended spiritual exploration |
| Sun Ra | *Heliocentric Worlds* | 1960s | Cosmic/spiritual vision |

**Key Quotation (Historical Context)**:
> "John Coltrane's 1965 album A Love Supreme is considered a landmark in the genre, establishing the approach of spiritual transcendence through jazz improvisation."  
*Source: Wikipedia - Spiritual Jazz*

## 2. THE 2015 SPIRITUAL JAZZ REVIVAL: KAMASI WASHINGTON & KENDRICK LAMAR BREAKTHROUGH

### The Epic Moment

**2015 marked the spiritual jazz revival's breakthrough**, with dual catalysts:

#### (A) Kamasi Washington: *The Epic* (May 5, 2015)

- **Album**: *The Epic* (3-hour opus, Brainfeeder Records)
- **Significance**: Widely celebrated as "assertive, unrelenting spiritual jazz"
- **Scale**: Choir, strings, extended suites, movement-style architecture
- **Reception**: Established Washington as clearest inheritor of Coltrane/Alice Coltrane tradition
- **Musical Impact**: The scale and orchestration functioned as a spiritual statement in itself

**Quotation**:
> "The Epic by Kamasi Washington (2015) is widely celebrated as assertive, unrelenting spiritual jazz, with its scale—choir, strings, extended suites, movement-style architecture—functioning as a spiritual statement in itself."  
*Source: Vice Magazine - "Kamasi Washington Takes Spiritual Jazz In a New Direction"*

#### (B) Kendrick Lamar: *To Pimp a Butterfly* (Collaboration, March 2015)

- **Context**: Kendrick Lamar solicited jazz master Kamasi Washington for collaborative saxophone/arrangement work
- **Band**: Featured Robert Glasper, Thundercat, Ambrose Akinmusire, and others
- **Genre Fusion**: Rap-G-funk-jazz fusion with politically charged themes
- **Cultural Impact**: Proved "intellectually stimulating music doesn't have to be underground"
- **Zeitgeist**: Album underscored the Black Lives Matter movement (2015 context)

**Key Quotation (Kamasi Washington on Impact)**:
> "That record changed music, and we're still seeing the effects of it. It went beyond jazz; it meant that intellectually stimulating music doesn't have to be underground."  
*Source: Complex Magazine - Interview with Kamasi Washington*

**Significance**:
> "The moment usually cited as the spiritual jazz revival's starting gun is 2015, for reasons that had as much to do with hip-hop as with jazz, when Kamasi Washington's appearance on Kendrick Lamar's To Pimp a Butterfly introduced his sound to an audience far beyond jazz's existing listeners."  
*Source: Wikipedia - Spiritual Jazz*

## 3. DINNER PARTY SUPERGROUP: FORMATION & IMPACT

### Composition & Origins

**NOT a James Blake collaboration** — the actual supergroup consists of:

| Member | Role | Background |
|--------|------|------------|
| **Robert Glasper** | Keyboardist | Neo-soul/jazz fusion pioneer; "Black Radio" series creator |
| **Terrace Martin** | Saxophonist | Los Angeles-based; Sounds of Crenshaw founder |
| **Kamasi Washington** | Saxophonist | Spiritual jazz leader; *The Epic* architect |
| **9th Wonder** | Hip-hop producer | North Carolina legend; MelloHype Records founder |

### Formation Story

- **Meeting Point**: Robert Glasper and Terrace Martin first met at jazz band camp in 1996
- **Catalyst**: Reunion during Glasper's R+R=NOW touring project
- **Intent**: "To explore undiscovered avenues of modern music"
- **Recording**: Chalice Recording Studios, Los Angeles (December 2019)

### Self-Titled Debut Album (July 10, 2020)

**Album Details**:
- **Label**: Sounds of Crenshaw / EMPIRE Records
- **Tracklist**: 7 original tracks
- **Grammy Nominations**: 14 nominations collectively among the group
- **Vocal Features**: Phoelix (Noname collaborator), credited on 5 of 7 tracks
- **Genre Blend**: Jazz, soul, R&B, hip-hop, and 1970s soul ("indebted to each, but adhering to their own rules")

**Album Concept**:
> "The band plays with notions of rap and jazz, R&B and '70s soul—indebted to each, but adhering to their own rules."  
*Source: Rolling Stone - Dinner Party Supergroup Coverage*

**Social/Political Context**:
> "The album is in part a protest album, masked as a palatable jazz fusion project, and though it was conceived before Breonna Taylor and George Floyd were killed by police, the music grapples with dealing with officers as a Black American."

## 4. DIFFERENCES FROM PREVIOUS JAZZ ERAS

### Hard Bop (1950s-1960s)

**Characteristics**:
- Gospel-like chords and blue-like melodies
- Dark yet soulful pitches
- Emphasis on small ensemble interaction
- R&B and blues-influenced grooves

**Contrast to Modern Spiritual Jazz**:
- Modern spiritual jazz uses larger orchestrations and extended forms vs. Hard Bop's intimate ensembles
- Modern approach emphasizes transcendence/social consciousness vs. Hard Bop's focus on soul/grooves
- Modern producers incorporate electronic elements vs. Hard Bop's acoustic foundation

### Modal Jazz (1960s)

**Characteristics**:
- Based on predetermined modes (musical scales)
- Slow mode changes (often 1-2 per chorus)
- Spacious, open canvas for melodic development
- John Coltrane's *Kind of Blue* model

**Contrast to Modern Spiritual Jazz**:
- Modern spiritual jazz extends modal thinking but adds free-form elements
- Modal Jazz was structured; modern spiritual jazz often abandons predetermined structures entirely
- Modal provided "frameworks for improvisation"; spiritual jazz treats improvisation as philosophical exploration

### Free Jazz / Avant-Garde (1960s-1970s)

**Characteristics**:
- Rejects predetermined harmonic structures
- Eliminates fixed tempos and forms (AABA, twelve-bar blues abandoned)
- Collective improvisation with equal voice for all players
- "Simultaneous conversations rather than soloist-over-rhythm-section"

**Contrast to Modern Spiritual Jazz**:
- Free Jazz was often perceived as chaotic/confrontational
- Modern spiritual jazz balances free improvisation WITH compositional intention (e.g., Washington's *The Epic* uses extended suites and deliberate architecture)
- Modern version incorporates diverse global traditions (Afrobeat, minimalism, Asian/African influences) where Free Jazz was more Western avant-garde centered
- Modern spiritual jazz has explicit social/spiritual mission vs. Free Jazz's emphasis on artistic freedom alone

**One-liner Synthesis**:
> "Spiritual jazz draws from free, avant-garde and modal jazz and thematically focuses on transcendence and spirituality, but adds intentional compositional structure, diverse global instrumentation, and explicit social consciousness."  
*Source: Wikipedia - Spiritual Jazz*
